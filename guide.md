<section class="guide-section">
  <h3>First of all: <strong>Immersion</strong> > Anki</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>The hard fact is that Anki alone won't make you <em>fluent</em> in Japanese (or any language).</li>
    <li>That's why <a target="_blank"
        href="https://www.memrise.com/blog/immersion-language-learning-the-best-way-to-learn-a-new-language-top-5-language-immersion-tips">immersion</a>
      needs to be the top priority. It actually teaches you how to <strong>use</strong> the language in the wild, so
      prioritize it above everything else.</li>
    <li>Anki decks should only play a supporting role, boosting your vocabulary, grammar and kanji and making all
      that immersion and overall experience a lot smoother and enjoyable.</li>
  </ul>
</section>
<section class="guide-section">
  <h3>Start with the <strong>Kana</strong> subdeck (if you have not learned kana yet)</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Learn the normal <em>Hiragana</em> and <em>Katakana</em>.</li>
    <li>Get the gist of the extended kana. There is no need to memorize them, just get used to how kana sounds
      combined with other kana.</li>
  </ul>
</section>
<section class="guide-section">
  <h3>Move on to the <strong>Core 10k</strong> subdeck</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Go through each subdeck (vocab and kanji) one by one.</li>
    <li>This can be considered a <em>JLPT deck</em> too, as it contains words and kanji that will appear on JLPT exams! <em>(read below)</em></li>
    <li><strong>Important note</strong>: The JLPT entries are based on lists curated from past exam papers <em>(up to 2011)</em>. This subdeck alone will not guarantee exam success and should be used alongside active learning, immersion and proper study methods!</li>
  </ul>
</section>
<section class="guide-section">
  <h3>Learn <strong>Grammar</strong></h3>
  <ul>
    <li>As with the Core 10k subdeck, go over each subdeck one at a time.</li>
    <li>Pair Core vocab with grammar for efficiency.</li>
    <li><em>(same important note from Core 10k subdeck)</em></li>
  </ul>
</section>
<section class="guide-section">
  <h3>The other subdecks</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Use all the <em>"reference material"</em> subdecks for a quick search when you need to look up radicals,
      kana, kanji etc.</li>
    <li>As for the <em>"ready-made mining material"</em> subdecks, all cards are suspended by default (if they are
      not, suspend them). Over time, unsuspend and learn the ones you find useful.</li>
  </ul>
</section>
<section class="guide-section">
  <h3>Tags</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Every card/note from every subdeck is tagged with their field of application, part of speech and other
      miscellaneous information regarding it.</li>
    <li>Use those tags to find the entries you need, especially in the <em>"ready-made mining material"</em>
      subdecks.</li>
  </ul>
</section>
